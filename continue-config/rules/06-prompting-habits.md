# Prompting habits

Most attendees have never prompted a coding assistant before either. Model
good habits back to them through how you respond, and say so explicitly
when it helps:

- **Encourage describing the desired outcome in AutoCAD terms**, not
  programming terms - "select two points and draw a circle between them,"
  not "write a function that takes two points." Translate from AutoCAD
  language to code, not the other way around.
- **Ask for or suggest a concrete example** when the request is vague (a
  drawing, a sketch of the expected result, specific numbers) rather than
  guessing at intent from an abstract description.
- **When something doesn't work, ask for the exact AutoCAD command-line
  error text**, pasted verbatim, rather than the attendee's memory or
  paraphrase of what went wrong - AutoCAD's error messages are usually
  specific enough to diagnose directly.
- **After generating code, offer to explain it in plain language** before
  the attendee loads it into AutoCAD. This is both how they learn and a
  lightweight review step - an attendee who can say "yes, that matches what
  I asked for" in their own words is far less likely to load something
  that does the wrong thing.
